# RULES.md — every challenge, as the referee plays it

Player-facing rules. No implementation, no API, no scores-as-code — just the game.
The referee enforces everything; the site only displays, times, and tallies.
Terms: see `CONTEXT.md`. Question-writing guidance: see `AUTHORING.md`.

## Password (v1)

One player per team sees a secret player name. They give a **one-word hint**; their partner
guesses. Teams alternate. 30 seconds per attempt. A hint may not be more than one word, a
number, a human name, or a club/city/country name. 1 point per solved name. If both teams
fail a name and agree, it is a draw (0–0). Most points after the run wins.

## Acting (v1)

A player acts out a secret player name with **no talking at all** while the teammate guesses:
45 seconds, max 3 guesses. On failure, the enemy team (both players together) gets 30 seconds
and exactly 1 guess off the same acting — a correct steal scores for them instead.
1 point per solved prompt. Most points after the run wins.

## Ihbid Sahh (v1)

Both teams receive the same question whose answer is a **number or a date** and write down
their answer within 30 seconds. Exact answer = 2 points, closer = 1 point. Same distance,
same answer, or both exact = draw (1–1, or 2–2 when both are exact). The referee judges who
was closer. Most points after the run wins.

## Offside (v1)

Both teams receive a rule (e.g. an Italian player) and write down any matching player name
within 10 seconds. A name nobody else wrote = 1 point; a duplicated name = 0. The referee
judges uniqueness and scores by hand. Most points after the run wins.

## Top 10 (v2)

A prompt names a top-13 ranking (e.g. all-time top league scorers). Teams name entries:
answering rank N scores N points (rank 1 = 1 … rank 10 = 10). Ranks 11/12/13 score
−1/−2/−3. Answering **all three** of 11+12+13 wipes the negatives and awards +6 instead
(final contribution of the trio is +6, not −6). The referee taps an answer to attribute it
to a team; points apply automatically. After 3 dead turns (no team answers) the room gets
a hint; the referee tracks dead turns. Most points after the run wins.

## Score Sheet (v2)

A famous match is announced (e.g. a World Cup final). Each team is assigned one side's
starting eleven (right side = team A, left side = team B, fixed): no substitutes,
no coaches — they are not part of the game. Teams take turns naming players; each correct
name = 1 point, attributed by tapping the player (that side's team scores). The full
lineups are visible to the referee from the start. Most points after the run wins.

## Risk (v3)

A board of **4 genres × 4 questions** (16 total). Genres are football categories: trophy,
competition, player, club, continent, nation, a specific season — anything similar. Inside
each genre: Easy = 5 pts, Medium = 10, Hard = 20, Very Hard = 40. **One of the 16 is a
hidden double-points question**, revealed only when chosen. Teams alternate picking a
question and have 30 seconds to answer. Most points at the end wins.
Each team holds 3 one-use cards, managed entirely by the referee (the site tracks nothing):
**Steal** (answer a question the other team failed, only before they answer), **Extra Time**
(+30 seconds), **Multiple Choice** (referee invents 4 options on the spot).

## Bank (v3)

Each team plays **3 rounds (6 rounds total)**. A round: 12 rapid-fire random questions,
120 seconds on the clock. Each correct answer chains: 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 … points. Saying
**bank** at any moment locks the current chain into the safe total. A wrong answer wipes
everything outside the bank. Round ends at 12 questions or timeout. Highest banked total wins.

## Ana Meen (v4)

The hidden subject is always a **player**. Five clues are given hard → easy, 60 seconds per
clue; a new clue appears if the player is not guessed. 1 point per solved player no matter
which clue cracked it. A challenge defaults to 3 subjects (adjustable 1 / 3 / 5 / 10),
teams alternating. The referee handles all turn rules; most points wins.

## Who-in-Picture (v4)

Both teams see a pre-match photo of 11 players and name them, alternating one guess per
player per turn. Each correct name = 1 point, attributed by referee tap. Mistakes attach to
**players, not teams**: 3 mistakes eliminates that player, the teammate continues. The
referee's screen holds the answers, so the photo is shared to the room (share button) while
names stay referee-only. Most points wins.
